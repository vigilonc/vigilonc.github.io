VIGILONC
Clinical decision support · Oncology pharmacotherapy

Pharmacological risk assessment for systemic therapy in NSCLC and melanoma

Vigilonc develops and validates a clinical-pharmacological risk score for therapy-limiting adverse events and combines it with drug interaction checks, dose verification and guideline-based treatment options for physicians.

Rationale

Therapeutic options are expanding rapidly. Treatment-limiting toxicity remains a major challenge.

Continuous drug development

New targeted agents, immune checkpoint inhibitors and combination regimens are approved at a high pace for NSCLC and melanoma, each with its own toxicity and interaction profile.

Vulnerable patient groups

Polypharmacy, drug–drug interactions, comorbidities and impaired organ function increase the risk of dose reduction, treatment discontinuation and hospitalisation. These patients are often under-represented in pivotal trials.

No integrated instrument

Risk factors, interaction data, dosing information and guideline recommendations are currently assessed separately. A standardised, validated tool that brings them together does not yet exist.

Platform

Four modules for a complete pre-treatment medication assessment

The platform is designed to support physicians and clinical pharmacists at the point of prescribing.

Risk stratification

A validated score assigns each patient to a low, intermediate or high risk group for therapy-limiting adverse events before treatment starts.

  • Clinical, laboratory and pharmacological predictors
  • Transparent contribution of each risk factor

Drug interaction check

Systematic screening of the complete medication list against the planned anticancer therapy, with severity grading and concrete management advice.

  • CYP450 and transporter-mediated interactions
  • QTc prolongation and additive toxicities
  • Food, supplements and over-the-counter drugs

Dose check

Verification of the planned dose against the approved label, organ function and interaction-driven adjustments.

  • Renal and hepatic dose adjustment
  • Label-based dose modification for interacting drugs
  • Dose reduction levels after adverse events

Guideline-based alternatives

When risk is high, the platform lists recommended treatment alternatives from European and national guidelines, adapted to the country of care.

ESMOS3 guideline (DE)Onkopedia (DE/AT/CH)NICE (UK)
  • National approval and reimbursement status

How a pre-treatment assessment could look

Patient68 years, ECOG 1
DiagnosisMetastatic NSCLC, EGFR exon 19 deletion
Planned therapyOsimertinib 80 mg once daily
Co-medicationCarbamazepine, citalopram, pantoprazole
eGFR48 mL/min/1.73 m²

Intermediate risk of therapy-limiting adverse events

LowIntermediateHigh
Major driver

Strong CYP3A4 inducer

Carbamazepine reduces osimertinib exposure and may compromise efficacy.

Contributor

QT-prolonging co-medication

Citalopram adds to the QTc effect of osimertinib.

Contributor

Age and reduced renal function

Age ≥ 65 and moderately reduced eGFR.

Avoid

Osimertinib + carbamazepine

Strong CYP3A4 induction lowers osimertinib exposure. Avoid the combination. If an inducer cannot be avoided, the label recommends 160 mg osimertinib once daily and returning to 80 mg three weeks after the inducer is stopped.

Monitor

Osimertinib + citalopram

Additive QTc prolongation. Obtain a baseline ECG and electrolytes, and repeat periodically during treatment.

No relevant interaction

Osimertinib + pantoprazole

Osimertinib absorption is not affected by gastric acid suppression.

Appropriate

Renal function

No dose adjustment for creatinine clearance of 15–89 mL/min. 80 mg once daily is appropriate for this patient.

Check

Interaction-driven adjustment

The dose depends on whether carbamazepine is continued. See the alternatives tab.

Option A

Adjust co-medication

Switch to a non-enzyme-inducing antiepileptic in consultation with neurology and keep osimertinib at 80 mg once daily.

Option B

Adjust osimertinib dose

If the antiepileptic cannot be changed, use the label-recommended osimertinib dose of 160 mg once daily.

Guideline

First-line options in EGFR-mutant NSCLC

European guidelines list osimertinib monotherapy, osimertinib with platinum–pemetrexed chemotherapy and amivantamab with lazertinib. Choice and availability depend on national approval and reimbursement.

Study design

Development and validation of the risk score

The score is developed from existing real-world data and reported according to the TRIPOD statement for clinical prediction models.

Step 1

Real-world cohorts

Retrospective analysis of patients with NSCLC and melanoma treated with modern systemic therapy.

Step 2

Predictor selection

Disease and treatment characteristics, co-medication, laboratory values and organ function.

Step 3

Model development

Statistical and machine-learning models in R and Python with internal validation, calibration and discrimination.

Step 4

External validation

Testing against VigiBase, the WHO global database of individual case safety reports.
